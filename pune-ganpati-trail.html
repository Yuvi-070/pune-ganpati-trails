Where this whole story begins

Shaniwar Wada

शनिवारवाडा

Built in 1732 as the seat of the Peshwas, this fortress-palace once governed the Maratha Empire from the very neighbourhood you're about to walk through. Fire gutted most of the interior two centuries ago, but the towering Delhi Darwaza gate and the garden ruins beyond still set the scene beautifully — especially in the soft light before 9 AM, when the gates aren't even open yet but the walk-up view might be the best part anyway.

Interior opens 9:00 AM–5:30 PM (₹20 entry) · exterior viewable any time · tip: loop back here once your trail is done — it'll be open by then

The 1st Manacha, and Pune's own Gramadevata

Kasba Ganpati

कसबा गणपती

Legend has it that Jijabai, mother of Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj, installed this idol in the 17th century after one surfaced near her home in Kasba Peth — and Pune has treated it as the city's own guardian deity ever since. By long-standing rule, no other Ganpati idol in the city begins its immersion procession until Kasba's has gone first.

6:00 AM–10:30 PM (closed to entry 12–4 PM; darshan from the doorway still works) · usual wait 15–30 min

India's first public Ganpati, 1892

Shrimant Bhausaheb Rangari Ganpati

श्रीमंत भाऊसाहेब रंगारी गणपती

A year before Lokmanya Tilak turned Ganeshotsav into a nationwide movement, a royal physician named Bhausaheb Rangari had already done it quietly, right here — installing the country's first sarvajanik (public) Ganpati idol as cover for secret meetings against British rule. The idol, carved showing Ganesha slaying a demon, hasn't been repainted or replaced since.

8:00 AM–1:00 PM, 4:00–9:00 PM · usual wait 15–25 min · tucked in a lane right before Dagdusheth

Pune's most magnificent Ganpati

Shrimant Dagdusheth Halwai Ganpati

श्रीमंत दगडूशेठ हलवाई गणपती

After sweet-maker Dagdusheth Halwai and his wife Lakshmibai lost their only son to plague in the 1890s, their spiritual guide told them to grieve by giving to the whole city instead. What they built — an idol wrapped in gold, jewels and constant devotion — is now one of India's richest and most visited temple trusts.

6:00 AM–11:00 PM · genuinely quick before 9 AM; by evening on a festival day it can run 3+ hours, so morning really is your friend here

The 2nd Manacha, reborn in fresh clay every year

Tambdi Jogeshwari

तांबडी जोगेश्वरी

Pune's second-ranked Manacha honours the city's own Gram-devi in a shrine with roots going back centuries. Almost alone among the grand mandals, Tambdi Jogeshwari still hand-sculpts a fresh clay idol every single year and immerses it faithfully — no fibreglass, no permanent murti, just an old promise kept season after season.

7:30 AM–1:00 PM, 3:30–10:00 PM · usual wait 20–40 min, longer once the evening lighting comes on

The 3rd Manacha, built by two communities

Guruji Talim

गुरुजी तालीम

Named for a 19th-century wrestling gymnasium that once stood here, this mandal was founded jointly by Hindu and Muslim families in 1887 — six years before Tilak's public Ganeshotsav even existed. It's one of the quietest, most sincere stops on the trail, and a reminder of exactly who built this festival's earliest traditions.

Open through the festival, roughly 6 AM–11 PM · usual wait under 20 min

Tulshibaug Ganpati

तुळशीबाग गणपती

Set inside one of Pune's oldest and most delightfully chaotic shopping lanes, this towering idol has watched over bargain hunters since the early 1900s. It was also the first Ganpati in the city sculpted in fibreglass rather than fresh clay, trading an annual rebuild for a murti that could be dressed in ever more elaborate jewellery.

9:00 AM–9:00 PM · usual wait 30–45 min · nicest before noon, the market itself gets loud after that

A different myth retold, every single year

Shanipar Mandal — this year's theme: "Shambhala"

शनिपार मंडळ

134 years in, this mandal still refuses to repeat itself. Every Ganeshotsav, Shanipar rebuilds its pandal around a different, often overlooked corner of Indian mythology — this year, across roughly 2,500 square feet of set design, the theme is Shambhala, the hidden and half-legendary kingdom of ancient lore.

Festival hours, typically morning–night · give it 20–30 min for the full set

The 5th Manacha, born in a newsroom

Kesariwada Ganpati

केसरीवाडा गणपती

The fifth and final Manacha closes the ceremonial circle inside the very building that once printed Kesari — Lokmanya Tilak's newspaper, and the launchpad for the public Ganeshotsav movement itself. A small attached museum keeps Tilak's memory alongside Bappa's.

Temple open 24 hours; the Tilak museum keeps separate daytime hours · usual wait 20–30 min, more if you linger inside

Supreme Sandwich Corner & Kaware Cold Drink House

Supreme Sandwich Corner

Somewhere north of sixty sandwich varieties, each stacked absurdly high and named like an inside joke — the Napoleon, the Bermuda Triangle, the Bombay Mix Special. Order without overthinking it.

Budhwar Peth outlet, right by Guruji Talim · 12:30–10:30 PM · closed Mondays

Kaware Cold Drink House

Right beside the Tulshibaug idol, this old-school parlour has been Pune's go-to Mastani stop for generations — somewhere between a milkshake and an ice-cream sundae. Rose, mango and pista are the classics. One Mastani, two spoons.

Tulshibaug, a 2-minute walk from Supreme Sandwich · 11:00 AM–10:00 PM

Get directions

Kaware opens earlier (11 AM) than Supreme Sandwich (12:30 PM) — if you arrive first, start with mastani and circle back for sandwiches.

Still have appetite for one more?

Two more worth knowing about, though neither quite fits the morning schedule above: Hutatma Babu Genu Mandal, the "Navsacha Ganpati" just behind Tulshibaug, only opens for darshan 6–10 AM — worth a peek only if you're running ahead of this itinerary. Chhatrapati Rajaram Mandal in Sadashiv Peth rebuilds a different famous temple as its pandal every year, but doesn't open until noon — a good reason to return one evening.

A few things worth knowing

This year's window

Ganeshotsav 2026 runs September 14–25. Go any day before the 25th — that's visarjan day, when the Manache Ganpati lead a procession through these same lanes and regular darshan gives way to the parade.

Start early

Leave Shaniwar Wada by 7–7:30 AM if you can. Dagdusheth's queue is genuinely reasonable at dawn and can run past three hours by evening.

Footwear & dress

Easy slip-on shoes — you'll leave them at the door a dozen times. Shoulders and knees covered is appreciated, especially at Kasba and Tambdi Jogeshwari.

Weather

Mid-September Pune is warm with the odd monsoon straggler. A light rain layer and a water bottle earn their place in the bag.

Photography

Phones are fine outdoors, but Dagdusheth's inner sanctum asks you to put the camera away and just look for a minute.

Cash

Small notes help at donation boxes, and at Tulshibaug's stalls if you get distracted by shopping — you will.
